Insights & recommendations

  1. Tighten the underwriting line between grade C and D. NPA rate roughly doubles from C (24.2%) to D (48.4%) — the steepest jump in the whole grade curve. Either raise the approval bar at that boundary or price D-and-below materially higher; treating C and D as adjacent risk tiers understates the gap.
  2. Move collections effort earlier, to DPD30, not DPD60–90. The roll-rate matrix shows cure probability collapsing non-linearly: 29.9% of DPD30 accounts self-cure to current, but only 4.7% do once an account reaches DPD90 — and 24.8% of DPD90 accounts roll straight to default the same month. Intervention has far more leverage before that cliff than after it.
  3. Don't score a recent vintage against a matured one. The newest cohorts look artificially clean only because they haven't aged long enough to default yet — 2026Q2 shows 0.6% cumulative default at month 5 versus ~5–6% for older vintages at the same age. Compare cohorts at equal months-on-book, never at equal calendar date.
  4. Layer loan purpose into pricing, not just grade. Medical (24.7%) and debt-consolidation (24.4%) loans run meaningfully higher NPA than venture (18.0%) or education (19.3%) loans at the same grade mix — grade alone is leaving risk on the table.

Underlying repayment history is simulated (the source dataset has no time series), calibrated so per-grade default rates and the overall ~22% base rate match the real credit-risk dataset. Read this as a demonstration of the analysis method, not as a live production portfolio.

Warehouse: DuckDB · 514,015 monthly repayment records across 32,571 loans · built on top of the credit-risk classifier project.
